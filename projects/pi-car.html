Pi Car Pro
Custom Gripper

An ongoing personal engineering project to redesign the manipulator on a Pi Car Pro mobile robotics platform. The goal is to move beyond the stock arm and develop a more capable, sturdy, and manufacturable gripper.

01 / Project Log

Documenting the project as it develops.

The project log is intentionally structured like an engineering notebook. Each entry records what I worked on, what I observed, and what those observations mean for the next design iteration.

08.22.26

Initial brainstorming

Today was the day I began brainstorming my ideas for the arm. The Pi Car Pro kit already comes with parts and assembly instructions for a grabber, but it only has two fingers. My goal is to improve the claw to have at least 4 fingers, and to have more stability and precision to pick up small objects such as toy balls.

I created a task list for this project to help me stay on track as time goes on. I did this because the kit did not come with a Raspberry Pi so I’m currently waiting on it to be shipped. In the meantime I want to maintain productivity because there is still a substantial amount of things to do while I wait.

  1. Assemble kit without electronic components: Since there will be a good amount of reverse engineering required to make my claw, I need to construct the stock version to familiarize myself with the parts and components I’ll be using. It will improve my prototyping to be aware of dimensions and mounting technology beforehand.
  2. Obtain 3D Model of Pi Car Pro: I will likely use preexisting parts in my design, and I will want to construct new parts based off of the size and geometry of these parts. Modeling and machining these will require a 3D model of the existing pieces.
  3. Begin improved grabber CAD: I will create different potential versions of the new grabber.
  4. Begin writing Python for the servos: Even without the microcontroller I can begin writing code that will later be used for the robot.
09.12.26

Platform assembly

Today I spent a few hours putting the robot and the arm together. Funny enough the most difficult part of assembling it was peeling off the protective film from each acrylic piece. Now that the robot is assembled I can analyze how the arm mounts onto the chassis and integrate that into my design.

I have noticed a few things about the chassis assembly that may be an issue in the future. The arm sits at the front of the chassis and is heavy enough that it slightly bends. Additionally, the instruction manual has the builder install a screw between a servo and the aluminum mount that holds it in such a way that the screw head forces the aluminum to bend. I believe this was an intentional design choice required to accommodate for the width of the base of the arm. I am going to take important notes of these things and aim to fix them in my final design.

09.16.26

Reverse engineering Pi Car parts

Unfortunately, I could not find a public official STEP file for the Pi Car Pro or any of its elements. I looked for unofficial, community-made files as well but I had no luck. I have started making the acrylic pieces first, measuring the cutouts with a ruler and modeling them in SolidWorks. Once I have each of these components made, I can make any new parts based off of those when I am customizing the gripper.

09.22.26

Reverse engineering Pi Car parts, continued

I continued reverse engineering the existing Pi Car Pro components so that I can build the custom gripper around the actual mounting geometry of the platform. This gives me a usable CAD reference for developing new parts instead of designing from assumed dimensions.

Future CAD screenshots and additional documentation can be added directly to this entry as the design develops.
