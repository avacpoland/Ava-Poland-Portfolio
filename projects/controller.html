01 / Requirements

Five criteria drove the design.

The team defined safety, cost-efficiency, compatibility, ease of use, and durability as the most important requirements. We tested the finished design against those criteria rather than evaluating it only by appearance.

02 / CAD

Complex curved surfaces, built from scratch.

I completed the controller's CAD design and produced multiple 3D-printed prototypes. The final enclosure housed a PCB, Arduino Uno R3, and eight push buttons.

03 / Iteration

The biggest problem was caused before the CAD was finished.

The PCB we ordered was significantly larger than expected. That forced the controller enclosure to grow and made it harder to keep the design ergonomic for the average user. Multiple prototype iterations helped us work around the constraint rather than discovering it only at the end.

WHAT I LEARNED

My first experience with Python made programming much less intimidating. I also learned practical hardware skills, manufacturing documentation, BOMs, safety criteria, and the importance of validating requirements throughout a project.
