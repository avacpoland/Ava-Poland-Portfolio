05 / CAD + FabricationOct 2023 — Apr 2024

RC Mustang
Project Postmortem

A senior-year project to build a remote-controlled drift car from scratch. I modified a Mustang mesh into a printable shell, designed a custom spoiler, and CNC-machined the aluminum chassis and electronics housing. The car was not fully completed.

STATUS
INCOMPLETE / LESSON LEARNED
TOOLS
Fusion 360 · CNC · 3D Printing
01 / Objective

Build an RC drift car around a custom body.

I modified a free Mustang model into a shell that could be 3D printed in four portions, then designed a custom spoiler and the aluminum chassis and electronics housing.

02 / What went wrong

The project taught me that the software workflow is part of the engineering problem.

The original body was a mesh, and I spent too much time trying to make that approach behave like a solid model. The project also exceeded my budget, and I did not complete the final assembly.

03 / Takeaway

Knowing when to abandon an approach is an engineering skill.

Looking back, I should have spent less time fighting the original mesh and instead built a simpler body around the constraints of the tools available. The project became a foundation for later CAD work and taught me that efficient engineering is often about choosing the right workflow—not just producing the right geometry.

WHAT I'M PROUD OF

I successfully created and integrated a custom spoiler, produced printable body sections, and CNC-machined major chassis components. More importantly, the project gave me the CAD experience that carried directly into later work.
